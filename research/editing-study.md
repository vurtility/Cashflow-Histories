# Editing study: what top business channels put on screen

**Method (9 Nov 2026).** For 8 high-performing videos from 8 channels, I pulled YouTube's seek-bar preview frames (one every 5–10s, 1,172 frames in total), classified every frame by type, and estimated cut rhythm from how often consecutive frames show a different shot. No videos were downloaded.

**Limits.**
- The mix figures are hand-classified estimates, accurate to about ±5–10 points.
- Shot lengths are modelled from sampling: if cuts land at random, P(same shot after Δ seconds) = e^(−Δ / average shot length). That's accurate to about ±30%.
- Exact frame-level cut timing needs the videos themselves (see the note at the end).

## 2. Cut rhythm (estimated average shot length)

| Channel | Sampling | Samples that changed shot | Est. average shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| ColdFusion | 10s | 96% | ~3.1s |
| Company Man | 5s | 82% | ~2.9s |
| Bloomberg Originals | 5s | 79% | ~3.2s |
| Logically Answered | 10s | 94% | ~3.6s |
| Wendover | 10s | 94% | ~3.6s |
| Johnny Harris | 5s | 69% | ~4.3s |
| Modern MBA | 10s | 84% | ~5.5s (charts hold longer) |
| MagnatesMedia | 10s | 83% | ~5.6s (title cards hold longer) |

**Takeaway:** the norm is a new image every **~3–4 seconds**. Charts and title cards hold for 5–8s, which pulls the average up. Your 1.5s minimum fits: quick cuts of about 1.5–2.5s for montages, about 3–4s as the default, and 5–8s for charts and documents the viewer needs to read.

## 3. What this means for Cashflow Histories

**The closest model is Logically Answered:** about two-thirds real footage of the story, low stock (~7%), and cards and screenshots for everything else. It's also the one channel that already meets your ≤10% stock rule. Most others are well above it: ColdFusion ~25%, Modern MBA ~28%, Company Man ~20%, Wendover ~20%. **Our rule is stricter than the genre's norm**, so the footage pipeline must find real material, not fall back on stock.

**Target mix for a faceless channel with an AI voice:**

| Type | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Real footage of the story | **40–50%** | News reports, the real people speaking (interviews, keynotes, earnings calls, hearings), brand TV ads, press-kit footage, real stores and locations. This is what makes an AI voice feel credible. |
| Archival | **5–15%** | Old photos, vintage ads, historic news, for origin stories. |
| Real voices (sound-up clips) | **5–10%** | 3–6 clips per episode, 3–12s each: the founder, CEO, analyst, franchisee or customer saying the key line in their own voice. The narration pauses for them. This is the strongest antidote to an AI voice. |
| Documents, screenshots & quotes | **12–15%** | Filings, headlines, menus, studies, plus quotes from **any** source shown as that source (article clipping, transcript, book page, press release, X/LinkedIn/Reddit post card), with the key line highlighted. Every serious channel does this, and it signals "sourced". |
| Charts & data | **12–18%** | Our numbers spine. Hold 5–8s each. |
| Text, quote & chapter cards | **5–8%** | Quotes when there's no clip of the person saying it; chapter breaks. |
| Generic stock | **≤10%** (hard cap) | Only for abstract ideas (money, contracts, crowds). |
| Custom animation | **≤5%** | Only when nothing real exists (e.g. the worked hypothetical). |
| Hollywood film clips | **0%** | MagnatesMedia's third of screen time is the highest copyright risk in the genre, and it reads as filler. |

## 4. Techniques worth borrowing

1. **One consistent treatment over all borrowed footage.** MagnatesMedia's green grade, Company Man's cut-outs on purple, Bloomberg's old-TV-set frame around archive news, Logically Answered's yellow chapter cards. → Our Ink Misprint treatment (two-ink tint, halftone, registration marks) does this job. It also makes mixed-quality clips look like one channel, and adds transformation for fair use.
2. **Headline screenshots with a highlighter.** MagnatesMedia (green), Bloomberg (orange on teal cards), Johnny Harris (yellow on studies and menus). → Pink highlighter swipes on halftoned article and filing crops.
3. **The real people, speaking.** Logically Answered and ColdFusion keep cutting to the actual CEOs (TV interviews, conferences, hearings). → For Subway: Fred DeLuca interviews, Peter Cancro interviews and keynotes, local-news franchisee interviews, Roark and Jersey Mike's IPO coverage.
4. **The brand's own ads.** Modern MBA, Company Man and Johnny Harris all cut in TV commercials and promo footage. → The $5 footlong jingle ad, Subway and Jersey Mike's spots.
4b. **Real logos everywhere they help recognition.** Logos on storefront shots, beside chart bars and in thumbnails (Logically Answered's Sprint and AT&T logos, Company Man's logo cut-outs). Use them to identify the company, never to imply endorsement.
5. **Cut-out images on brand colour.** Company Man's cheapest repeatable device: logo or product cut-outs on a solid background. → Products and logos on our pink, blue or yellow ink fields, for beats with no footage.
6. **Archival framed as archival.** Bloomberg's TV-set frame, Company Man's black-and-white photos. → A "reprint" treatment (heavier halftone, paper grain) for anything historic.
7. **Charts hold; footage moves.** Data scenes sit for 5–8s, while footage cuts every 2–4s. Alternating the two creates rhythm.
8. **No sponsor segments.** Wendover loses ~12% and Johnny Harris ~10% of screen time to sponsors; we don't.

## 5. Next: the footage pipeline (Subway first)

- **Shot list:** each narration line becomes shots of ≥1.5s, using the type targets above.
- **Sourcing, safest first:** public documents and filings → Wikimedia, the Library of Congress and government sources → press kits and newsrooms → Internet Archive (vintage ads and news) → news and TV clips, the brand's own ads, social posts as screenshots → stock (≤10%).
- **Asset log:** source, licence or fair-use reason, and seconds used per source, plus checks on the stock percentage and the longest clip from any one source.
- **Treatment and assembly** in Remotion, timed to David's voiceover.

**Note on exact cut timing:** measuring individual cuts frame by frame needs the actual video files. Fetching those needs a YouTube downloader, which my sandbox blocked. If you want frame-exact numbers, approve the downloader. The 3–6s estimates above are good enough to start editing.
